01 / My perspective

Engineering matters when it reaches the field.

I believe the most useful technology begins with understanding the people, places, and problems it is meant to serve.

My background is in Electrical Engineering at Universitas Brawijaya. Over time, I moved from robotics and embedded systems into computer vision, underwater data collection, and marine conservation technology.

At LINI Foundation, I work on robotic systems and AI models that support coral reef monitoring. My work in Les Village also brings me into regular contact with local conservation practitioners, and with children learning English through community classes. Those experiences keep me thinking beyond what a model can predict — toward what people can actually use.

Behind the projects

AI for Coral & Reef Fish MonitoringModel development / Current work+

Problem

Underwater imagery contains rich information, but manually identifying coral forms and reef fish in large collections takes time and specialized knowledge.

My technical work

Developing and iterating YOLOv8-based computer vision workflows, with seven coral life-form classes and roughly 20 reef fish species in the broader model development scope.

Where it could go

Model-assisted reviewing, data summaries, and accessible interfaces that support — rather than replace — ecological experts and community monitoring.

Current status: model development and experimentation. Validation results and a public demonstration should be added only when you have figures and media you can share.
Underwater ROV & 3D Reef MappingRobotics / Image acquisition+

Problem

Repeatedly documenting underwater sites requires reliable data collection in conditions that are less predictable than a laboratory or pool.

My technical work

Working with robotic platforms, ROS, sensor integration, camera systems, navigation experiments, and photogrammetric reconstruction of reef scenes.

Field application

Using underwater images and mapped environments to support documentation and comparison of marine restoration and monitoring sites.

Team project at LINI Foundation. Add annotated architecture diagrams, 3D model previews, and specific responsibilities when ready to publish.
Robot-Assisted Seagrass PlantingExploration / Restoration pilot+

Problem

Seagrass restoration can require labor-intensive underwater work, motivating experiments with different approaches to planting and monitoring.

Experiment

An early-stage pilot explored technology-assisted planting using Thalassia hemprichii, including a trial involving 80 shoots in April 2024.

What it means

An exploratory engineering effort — not evidence of proven long-term restoration success. It helped identify practical constraints and potential applications.

Pilot status: experimental. The main current robotics focus remains coral reef monitoring and documentation.

04 / Where I want to grow

Learning & research direction — not a deployed system

Beyond one data type: multimodal AI.

One underwater image tells a story. Combining multiple types of environmental data could help us understand a much bigger picture.

I want to learn how models can connect visual observations with mapping, underwater sound, and other ecological evidence. I'm especially interested in designing systems that turn those different signals into understandable insights for researchers and local monitoring teams.

05 / People & place

Learning from Les Village.

Some of the most valuable lessons I've learned about technology come from spending time with people doing conservation on the ground.

In Les Village, North Bali, I join ongoing reef-monitoring activities and learn alongside local practitioners. I also take part in English learning activities for children from the community. These experiences help me see conservation as both an environmental and a human challenge.

I'm interested in building digital tools that are not just technically impressive, but understandable, affordable, and useful within the context of a local community. That means listening first, validating assumptions, and making room for community feedback.

06 / A tool I want to build

Concept / Not yet launched

AI tools should be usable outside the lab.

Imagine a simple monitoring workspace where local teams can upload reef photos, review model suggestions, and track observations over time.

This is an early product direction, not an already functioning platform. The next step would be to talk with community users, define the smallest useful workflow, and validate both the interface and the underlying models.

Make data collection easier

Simple uploads, clear location and date records, and low-friction interfaces.

Keep people in the loop

AI predictions are suggestions that reviewers can inspect and correct.

Return useful information

Understandable summaries, exportable records, and design for limited connectivity.
