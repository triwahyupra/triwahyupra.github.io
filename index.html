02 / Computer vision · real evidence

I build models—and show the work behind them.

These are outputs from my coral and reef-fish detection experiments, together with the data preparation and evaluation steps used during development.

Project 01 · Coral life-form detection

From annotated coral imagery to model predictions.

My experiments with underwater object detection include labeling coral life forms, preparing and augmenting image datasets, comparing YOLO variants, and reviewing detection outputs. The project explores how computer vision can assist coral reef monitoring—not replace ecological experts.

Project 02 · Reef fish detection

Expanding from corals to reef fish.

Fish detection presents different challenges: moving subjects, variable underwater visibility, occlusion, and differences between species. This demonstration illustrates my ongoing exploration of AI-assisted biodiversity monitoring.

I want to keep improving dataset quality, generalization, error analysis, and model evaluation so the outputs become useful beyond a development environment.

03 / Field robotics & mapping

Building the robot is one challenge. Using it at sea is another.

My fieldwork involves robot equipments, boats, dynamic ocean conditions, image collection, iterative troubleshooting, and collaboration with the people supporting each deployment.

Project 03 · Reef mapping

Underwater imagery → reconstructed 3D reef.

Using underwater imagery captured during fieldwork, I work with photogrammetric reconstruction to create 3D representations of reef environments. These models can support documentation, visualization, and comparisons in marine monitoring when collected and interpreted appropriately.

Project 04 · Experimental restoration

Exploring what an underwater robot could plant.

Our pilot work explored robot-assisted planting of Thalassia hemprichii seagrass species in Mertasari Beach, Denpasar. I contributed to ROV deployment, field piloting, monitoring, and troubleshooting alongside teammates and boat operators which is from fisher community.

Deployment→Remote piloting→Planting trial→Documentation

04 / Les Village · Bali

Conservation doesn't happen inside a laboratory alone.

The community context matters as much as the technical solution. Field experiences in Les Village shape how I think about making AI and robotics useful, accessible, and relevant.

Field monitoring with community members · Les Village, North Bali

Learning together

Working alongside the people closest to the reef.

I take part in coral reef monitoring and field activities with local conservation practitioners and members of the fishing community. Working with boat crews, staff, and volunteers showed me that strong tools must fit the realities of fieldwork.

Through these experiences, I want to move beyond building technology that works in demonstrations and toward tools that communities can use with confidence, appropriate training, and ownership.

“The more we understand the ocean, the better we can protect it.”

Education & access

Learning English with the next generation.

I help with English learning activities for children from local fishing families. These experiences reinforce the importance of sharing knowledge in approachable, practical ways.

Science communication

Making conservation work understandable.

At the LINI Aquaculture and Training Center, I've introduced visitors to nursery activities, artificial reef structures, and reef restoration programmes—connecting technical work with public understanding.

05 / What I want to learn and build next

Toward multimodal AI and useful community tools.

I've built and tested computer vision systems. My next ambition is to learn how to combine multiple forms of ocean data—and turn those findings into tools that are easier to use outside research labs.

Learning & research direction.

Connecting the ocean's different clues.

Underwater images can show visible organisms; 3D maps describe spatial structure; audio may capture marine soundscapes; and eDNA may provide additional evidence of species presence. I want to explore how multimodal AI could combine these signals responsibly, and how the resulting insights might be useful for conservation teams, researchers and coastal communities.
